# Citation ledger

Every regulation, standard, clause number, deadline, threshold, percentage, role, and effective date asserted in "Compiled AI, industry by industry" (dated 2026-09-01), checked on 2026-09-02. Each row carries the claim as the document states it, a status, the URL that was actually fetched, a short verbatim excerpt from what was fetched, and a note.

Statuses: VERIFIED (a fetched copy of the source supports the claim as stated), PARTIAL (the requirement exists but the citation, scope, or wording is off; the note says which part), INCORRECT (the fetched source contradicts the claim), UNVERIFIABLE (paywalled standard or blocked source; the note says what could be confirmed).

Totals across the three parts: 97 claims. VERIFIED 62, PARTIAL 15, INCORRECT 1, UNVERIFIABLE 19.

One caveat governs the whole ledger. Every primary host was blocked by the network policy of the session that produced it: eCFR, govinfo, uscode.house.gov, the Federal Register, FINRA, CMS, NERC, the FCC, EUR-Lex, the ABA, ISO, and the rest. Checks used verbatim copies on GitHub: government-published source repositories where they exist (the Department of Justice's foia.gov site source; GSA's FAR and DFARS publishing repositories) and third-party mirrors of public-domain text otherwise, matched across two mirrors where possible. Each part states its evidence tiers. Nothing was marked VERIFIED from memory. The ledger should be re-run against the primary hosts from an unrestricted network; the fetched URLs and excerpts make that a mechanical job.

The ledger is in three parts by industry group, as produced.

### Discrepancies (most important first)

1. **Legal — Rule 1.7(b) misstated (G1).** The document lists two waiver conditions; the rule has four, all cumulative. Missing: (b)(2) "the representation is not prohibited by law" and (b)(3) no "assertion of a claim by one client against another client represented by the lawyer in the same litigation or other proceeding before a tribunal". With the document's "only if" framing, a compiled rule would let consent cure non-consentable conflicts.
2. **Logistics — deadline cited to the wrong part (L1).** The 10-working-day entry-summary deadline is 19 CFR 142.12(b) ("within 10 working days after the time of entry"), and the Form 7501 requirement is 19 CFR 142.11(a). Part 141 ("Entry of Merchandise") holds the definitions (141.0a) and "time of entry" (141.68) but not the deadline.
3. **Logistics — "summary clock is tracked from arrival" is wrong (L4).** The clock runs from the "time of entry", which by default is CBP's release authorization (141.68(a)(1)); arrival is the trigger only in the 141.68(a)(3) case.
4. **Telecom — validation rule conflated with 47 CFR 52.36 (C5).** 52.36 lists 14 required LSR data fields plus an optional passcode; the four-field validation limit (TN, account number, ZIP, passcode) is from FCC 10-85 / the NANC flows (75 FR 35315, June 22, 2010), not from the codified text. The document should cite both and stop calling the four-field limit "the rule".
5. **Logistics — "name, use, and material" is not the HTS test (L9).** GRI 1 makes classification turn on the terms of the headings *and* the section/chapter notes, then GRI 2–6 and the Additional U.S. Rules (use provisions). Compiling headings as name/use/material conditions would drop the legally binding notes.
6. **Telecom — one-business-day rule stated without its exception (C1).** 52.35(a) adds "unless a longer period is requested by the new provider or by the customer".
7. **Technology — Art. 15(4) overstated (T5).** The text limits the copy right so as not to "adversely affect the rights and freedoms of others"; it does not prescribe withholding as the response.
8. **Legal — Rule 1.10 stated without exceptions (G5).** (a)(1) personal-interest conflicts, (a)(2) screened lateral hires, and (c) waiver under 1.7 are omitted from the "who counts" definition; state versions also differ (Kansas lacks (a)(2)).
