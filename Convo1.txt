## What I meant about Cyc and the Semantic Web

**Cyc** began in 1984 under Doug Lenat. The plan was to write down what an ordinary person knows as logic assertions in a language called CycL so a machine could reason over them. The writing was done by people. An engineer reads a sentence of English, decides which existing symbols it involves or coins new ones, and types the assertion. Forty years and millions of hand-typed assertions later, it did not reach the coverage of ordinary knowledge. The cost was one human act per fact, and the number of facts is unbounded.

**The Semantic Web** was proposed in 2001 by Berners-Lee, Hendler, and Lassila. The plan was that web pages would carry machine-readable statements in RDF using shared vocabularies in OWL, so software could reason over the web the way people read it. The statements were to be written by page authors, by hand, in ontologies that also had to be hand-agreed. Almost nobody did it. The web of data stayed tiny next to the web of documents.

The parts that worked are the instructive ones. DBpedia was built by mechanically extracting Wikipedia infoboxes, which are tables, into triples. Schema.org got adoption because it is a small fixed vocabulary. Both are your door two: structured data in, a small fixed mapping, no human per fact.

"Hand-built mapping" means that in both projects the step from English to symbols could only be done by a person, one fact at a time, because nothing else could read English. The symbolic systems starved for input. That diagnosis is Tunstall-Pedoe's, from the second essay, and his fix is that LLMs now do the translation at scale, checked at rest.
